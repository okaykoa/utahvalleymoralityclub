---
title: "Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong"
author: J.L. Mackie (1977)
category: For the Young Reader
order: 1
---
Many of our members return to this one first. Mackie writes with the
plainspoken confidence of a good neighbor explaining how things really
are &mdash; and what he concludes is that our moral talk means exactly what
it appears to mean, a claim about how things really stand, and that
every such claim is false, there being no such facts for it to be
about. His "argument from queerness" asks what kind of fact a moral
fact would have to be, and invites us to notice how little sense that
fact makes once examined closely. A bracing start for anyone who
assumed good character rests on solid ground.
