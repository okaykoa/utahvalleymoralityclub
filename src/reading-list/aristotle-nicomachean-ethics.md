The book our Charter has been quoting without credit. Aristotle's claim
is that virtue is not a fact we discover or a feeling we happen to have,
but a skill we acquire the way a lyre player acquires theirs &mdash; by
doing the thing, badly at first, in the company of others who already do
it well. We become temperate by practicing temperance, and just by doing
just acts, long before we understand either. Every later entry on this
list that talks about character owes him a debt; most do not repay it
in full.
